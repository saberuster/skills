# Candidate rubric

Keep one claim per candidate, with the future decision it changes. In compact
notes record its kind, scope and conditions, evidence and support level,
existing coverage and proposed disposition, authoritative destination and
reading path, and validity or recheck conditions. Evidence includes the source
role, available locator or safe excerpt, and observed outcome; label inferred
or incomplete support. Keep relevant dates, versions, and exceptions. Unknown
scope stays unresolved. No fixed format or numeric score is required.

## Evidence and durability

- One explicit statement can establish a scoped preference or decision, not
  verify a technical result. Implementation facts are not automatically policy;
  an agent suggestion needs acceptance or evidence to become an obligation.
- Retain prerequisites, action, outcome, and limits. Proposed fixes and assistant
  success claims are not verification. One successful run supports its observed
  conditions, not a universal rule; association does not establish cause.
- Use recurrence in this conversation or user-provided reuse evidence. Do not
  infer unseen history, treat repeated retries as independent validation, or
  infer approval from silence. Reviewer agreement does not raise the number
  of real observations. Do not infer sensitive traits or preferences.
- Keep contrary evidence and exceptions; do not invent probabilities or promote
  knowledge after a fixed count.
- Skip generic advice, temporary task state, one-off details, unverified
  guesses, secrets, and one-time permission. A handoff is a separate artifact.
  Prefer references to maintained sources over duplicating discoverable facts.
- Stable paths or version-specific knowledge can be useful when qualified by
  scope and a recheck condition. Do not turn a transient SHA, local path, or
  business rule into a universal requirement.

## Reconciliation

First check whether apparently conflicting claims have the same scope and
conditions. Preserve distinct valid cases instead of choosing by recency.
Update descriptive facts with evidence; replacing preferences needs a clear
new preference in the same scope. Removing or weakening an obligation needs
an explicit superseding decision, withdrawal, or established expiry. Age,
low usage, implementation drift, or a higher model score is insufficient.

| Disposition | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| `add` | Supported, useful knowledge has no existing home within the target |
| `amend` | Merge equivalent knowledge or clarify conditions, wording, or placement |
| `supersede` | Evidence and authority establish what replaces an old claim |
| `covered` | Clear, applicable existing content already expresses the lesson |
| `discard` | The candidate lacks durable value or adequate support |
| `unresolved` | Scope, access, conflicting authority, or necessary evidence prevents a decision |

Unresolved alternatives must not both become active rules. Explain the conflict
and preserve the existing source unless a valid correction is established.
Keep pending candidates in the response; persist a pending item only when
requested, in a location excluded from normal instruction loading. Do not
place low-confidence staging notes in automatically loaded memory.

On repeated reflection, compare scope, topic, conditions, and existing content.
No new evidence or meaningful content difference means no write, including no
timestamp-only diff. Add supporting evidence only when it changes the useful
record, and keep superseded or expired material clearly historical if retained.
